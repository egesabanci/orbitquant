# P1.4 — Outlier-Aware Hadamard Channel Permutation

**Status:** Implemented and benchmarked (pure NumPy, no model/GPU).
**Files:** `baseline/p14_outlier_perm.py`, `baseline/run_p14.py`
**Run:** `python3 -m baseline.run_p14 --d 64 --nrot 2000`

## Purpose

Before applying a Hadamard transform, reorder channels so persistent outlier
channels are spread across different butterfly groups. This keeps the speed of
FWHT while reducing the chance that a few large channels dominate a local mixing
path (grounded in RotateKV's channel reordering with FWHT-style rotations).

## Method

Calibration on 512 synthetic unit vectors with 8 fixed outlier channels (4×
gain): channels ranked by mean |x_i| (recovers the true outlier set), top-8
placed at bit-reversed positions (one outlier per FWHT butterfly group of size
8), non-outliers fill the rest. The permutation is fixed post-calibration;
sign flips are redrawn per trial. Rotation = sign-flip Hadamard (1 round)
composed with the permutation, as Rotation objects. Compared against no-perm
and random-perm on the rotation checks (beta_ks, rms_corr, mse).

## Results — d=64, nrot=2000, b=2, seed=0

```
fixed x=outlier (fixed adversarial, clustered outliers)
rotation        beta_ks  rms_corr      mse   rt_s
no-perm          0.0413    0.0223   0.3702  0.162
random-perm      0.0080    0.0217   0.3465  0.168
outlier-perm     0.0514    0.0225   0.3005  0.164

fixed x=e1 (degenerate for permutations)
rotation        beta_ks  rms_corr      mse   rt_s
no-perm          0.3398    0.0227   0.0396  0.159
random-perm      0.3397    0.0216   0.0396  0.203
outlier-perm     0.3400    0.0222   0.0396  0.169
```

## Findings

1. **Outlier-aware permutation gives the lowest MSE on outlier vectors:**
   0.3005 vs 0.3702 (no-perm) and 0.3465 (random-perm). The designed
   energy-spread mechanism works: it reduces the peak rotated coordinate
   (0.303 vs 0.387/0.529), which is the mechanism behind the MSE win.

2. **beta_ks is not improved** (0.0514 vs 0.0413 no-perm, 0.0080 random-perm).
   The permutation helps MSE by spreading energy but doesn't improve the
   coordinate distribution fit for the outlier vector. rms_corr (~0.022) is
   non-discriminating here (the sign-flip already decorrelates).

3. **x=e1 is degenerate for all permutations** (beta_ks ~0.34, mse 0.0396
   identical): H·P·e1 has flat |coords| for any P, so permutations can't
   distinguish on e1. The outlier vector is the right test input.

## Implications

- Outlier-aware permutation (P1.4) is validated for its stated purpose:
  reducing MSE on outlier-heavy vectors by spreading outlier channels across
  butterfly groups. On matched data it beats both no-perm and random-perm.
- It does not fix the distribution fit (beta_ks); that is the job of more
  mixing rounds (P0.2: hdhdh) or a hybrid rotation (P3.5).
- Cheap to deploy: the permutation is a fixed calibration-time table, no
  per-vector metadata (P0.5-friendly).
