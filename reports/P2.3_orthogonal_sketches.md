# P2.3 — Orthogonalized Residual Sketches

**Status:** Implemented and benchmarked (pure NumPy, no model/GPU).
**Files:** `baseline/p23_orthogonal.py`, `baseline/run_p23.py`
**Run:** `python3 -m baseline.run_p23 --d 64 --nrot 2000`

## Purpose

QJL reports practical gains from orthogonalizing JL rows (QJL Sec 4.1). P2.3
applies the same idea to TurboQuant's residual QJL stage, comparing iid Gaussian
rows, orthogonalized Gaussian rows, and structured orthogonal rows. No extra
stored bits — but scaling/unbiasedness must be verified because orthogonalized
rows are not literally iid Gaussian samples.

## Method

Fixed x=e1, y=all_equal (true ⟨y,x⟩ = 0.125), independent sketches per trial.
QJL estimator scale/m · Σ_i sign(⟨S_i,x⟩)⟨S_i,y⟩ with scale = ||x||·sqrt(pi/2)
for iid Gaussian rows, ||x||/E|u_1| for QR-orthonormalized rows (sphere
marginal; the per-row constant differs from the Gaussian one — sqrt(2/pi) vs
~sqrt(2/(pi·d)) — without which the orth estimator is biased down ~8x).

## Results — d=64, nrot=2000, m=64, seed=0

```
   sketch rows        bias    variance
           iid     -0.0101     0.02528
          orth      0.0012     0.00896
  variance ratio iid/orth = 2.821

variance vs sketch dimension m:
     m     iid var    orth var   ratio
    16     0.09381     0.07956   1.179
    32     0.04863     0.03270   1.487
    64     0.02433     0.00840   2.895
```

Robustness: high-nrot (seed 7, n=20000): ratio 2.805; seed spread (1,3):
both estimators unbiased within ~0.003.

## Findings

1. **Orthogonalized sketch rows cut estimator variance by ~2.8x at m=d=64**
   (0.00896 vs 0.02528) with negligible bias. The reduction grows with m
   (1.18x at m=16 → 2.9x at m=64), consistent with the orthogonal rows removing
   the redundant directions that iid Gaussian rows sample multiple times.

2. **Both constructions are unbiased** (orth bias 0.0012; the seed-0 iid bias
   -0.0101 is a heavy-tail finite-sample draw, not systematic — confirmed by the
   seed spread and high-nrot runs).

3. **Orthogonalized rows require m ≤ d** (at most d orthonormal rows in R^d),
   enforced with a clear error. This is a real constraint: for m > d the iid
   sketch is the only option, but the variance-ratio benefit is largest near
   m=d anyway.

4. **The sphere-marginal scaling constant is essential.** The per-row constant
   for orthonormal rows differs from the Gaussian one; using the Gaussian
   constant biases the orth estimator down ~8x. This was caught and fixed during
   development (verified: formula 0.100126 vs Monte Carlo 0.100116).

## Implications

- Orthogonalized residual sketches (P2.3) are validated: ~2.8x variance
  reduction at no extra stored bits, unbiased, and the benefit grows with m.
- This directly improves the residual stage of TurboQuant_prod and combines
  with P2.2 (structured sketches) and P2.4 (variable-size m).
- The m ≤ d constraint is the only limitation; for typical d=64/128 heads this
  is not binding.
