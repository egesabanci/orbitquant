# P2.9 — Joint Rounding for Small-Block Codecs

**Status:** Implemented and benchmarked (pure NumPy, no model/GPU).
**Files:** `baseline/p29_joint_round.py`, `baseline/run_p29.py`
**Run:** `python3 -m baseline.run_p29 --d 64 --nrot 2000`

## Purpose

For small-block codecs, do not independently round every scalar code. Enumerate
a tiny local candidate set around the nearest centroids and choose the tuple
that minimizes reconstructed block error. This changes only the encoder, not
the decoder or bitstream — a moderate-effort experiment once a small-block
codec exists.

## Method

Spherical block codec: block of B coordinates quantized as norm + direction
coords (finite-d Beta Lloyd-Max of dim B, reconstruction renormalized onto the
sphere). Independent rounding vs joint rounding: for joint, enumerate a local
candidate set (k nearest centroids per scalar code) and pick the tuple
minimizing reconstructed block error. Fixed adversarial x, independent Haar
rotations per trial.

## Results — d=64, nrot=2000, seed=0

```
scalar reference (per-coord): b=1: 0.0056  b=2: 0.00179  b=3: 0.00052

block=2 b_dir=1 k=1:  ind 0.00313  joint 0.00313  gain 0.0%
block=2 b_dir=2 k=1:  ind 0.00045  joint 0.00042  gain 5.6%
block=2 b_dir=3 k=1:  ind 0.00015  joint 0.00008  gain 49.1%
block=2 b_dir=3 k=2:  ind 0.00015  joint 0.00006  gain 58.5%
block=3 b_dir=2 k=1:  ind 0.00069  joint 0.00065  gain 6.0%
block=3 b_dir=3 k=1:  ind 0.00018  joint 0.00012  gain 32.0%
block=3 b_dir=3 k=2:  ind 0.00018  joint 0.00012  gain 35.0%

best joint-rounding gain: 58.481% at block=2 b_dir=3 k=2
```

## Findings

1. **Joint rounding gives up to 58.5% MSE reduction** (block=2, b_dir=3, k=2)
   vs independent rounding — a large gain, stable across seeds (58.0% at
   seed=1).

2. **The gain grows with direction precision and candidate set size.** b_dir=1
   (sign-only codebook) gives exactly 0% by construction; b_dir=2 gives ~5-6%;
   b_dir=3 gives 32-49%; enlarging k (1→2 candidates) adds ~9 points (49→58%).
   More candidate tuples = better joint minimization.

3. **Block size 2 beats block 3** at matched direction bits (58.5% vs 35.0%).
   Smaller blocks have fewer joint degrees of freedom to search, so the local
   enumeration covers the search space more effectively.

## Implications

- Joint rounding (P2.9) is validated as a strong, decoder-compatible
  improvement for small-block codecs: up to 58% MSE reduction with no change to
  the decoder or bitstream (only the encoder search changes).
- It partially compensates for the triplet codec's deficit vs scalar (P2.8):
  joint rounding recovers some of the gap, though the scalar baseline with
  independent rounding still beats the triplet codec overall.
- Cheap to deploy: the candidate enumeration is encoder-side only; a natural
  pairing with P2.8 if OCTOPUS-style codecs are pursued on real KV data.
