# P2.6 — Importance-Weighted Coordinate Bit Allocation

**Status:** Implemented and benchmarked (pure NumPy, no model/GPU).
**Files:** `baseline/p26_importance.py`, `baseline/run_p26.py`
**Run:** `python3 -m baseline.run_p26 --d 64 --nrot 2000`

## Purpose

After rotation, coordinates are close to independent but may not be equally
important for real attention. P2.6 estimates per-coordinate sensitivity and
allocates more bits to coordinates that matter. Critically, per P0.5, the
allocation/layout metadata must be **paid** in the byte budget — this benchmark
implements that honestly.

## Method

Calibration estimates per-coordinate sensitivity over independent rotations.
Two honest readings:
- **A. Fixed calibration mask:** the allocation pattern is computed ONCE from
  the calibration sensitivity and applied to every trial; nothing is signaled
  per vector — matched budget with uniform, no side info.
- **B. Per-vector adaptive:** the pattern is recomputed per trial from the
  realized importance w=y²; it must be signaled to the decoder at
  S = d·ceil(log2(bmax+1)) bits, charged against the budget.

## Results — d=64, nrot=2000, budget=128, bmax=4, seed=0

```
  signaling cost of a per-vector allocation pattern: S = 192 bits
  calibration (64 rotations): mean sensitivity = 0.01562 (1/d), max/min = 2.13
    -> coordinates exchangeable under Haar; allocation driven by w_i = y_i^2

uniform allocation     b=2  budget=128 bits  mse = 0.11436

A. fixed calibration mask (no per-vector side info):
   budget=128 bits (same as uniform)  mse = 0.11418
   gain vs uniform: 1.002x (better)

B. per-vector adaptive: signaling S exceeds budget for any b>=1 per-coordinate;
   not comparable at this budget.
```

## Findings

1. **The fixed calibration mask is a no-op under Haar** (1.002x — within noise).
   Coordinates are exchangeable under Haar rotation (mean sensitivity = 1/d
   exactly), so no fixed mask can single out important coordinates. This is a
   measured confirmation that within-vector importance weighting has nothing to
   exploit after a good rotation — consistent with P1.6.

2. **Per-vector adaptive allocation is NOT viable at realistic budgets.**
   The 192-bit signaling cost (d=64, bmax=4) exceeds the entire 128-bit
   quantizer budget, so the honest comparison is impossible at this budget —
   the signaling alone costs 1.5x the whole uniform budget. An earlier version
   of this benchmark reported a 1.67x gain by silently excluding these 192
   bits; that was an **oracle result** and has been corrected (this is the
   honest accounting P0.5 requires).

3. **The allocator mechanism itself is sound** (from P1.6's mechanism check:
   with genuinely unequal coordinate variance, adaptive allocation beats
   uniform by 4.3%). The issue is not the allocator — it is that Haar rotation
   equalizes coordinates and that per-vector signaling is expensive.

## Implications

- Importance-weighted allocation (P2.6) in its per-vector form is **not
  deployable** at realistic bit budgets: the allocation metadata (P0.5) is too
  expensive. This is a genuine negative result that the byte-accounting
  discipline surfaced.
- The viable form is a fixed, calibration-derived mask shared across all
  tokens — but under Haar that mask is ~uniform, so it buys nothing here.
  Any real gain would require coordinates with genuinely unequal importance
  (e.g. outlier channels pre-rotation, which P1.4's permutation addresses).
- Recommendation: pursue importance weighting only in settings with measured
  coordinate imbalance (outlier channels), and always via fixed calibration
  masks, never per-vector patterns.
