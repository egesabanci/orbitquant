# P1.5 — Attention-Sink / Outlier-Token Protection

**Status:** Implemented and benchmarked (pure NumPy, no model/GPU).
**Files:** `baseline/p15_protected.py`, `baseline/run_p15.py`
**Run:** `python3 -m baseline.run_p15 --d 64 --nrot 2000 --n 1000`

## Purpose

Keep a tiny subset of tokens at higher precision: attention sinks, system/prefix
tokens, and very recent tokens. The protected pool must be small enough that the
memory ratio barely moves. Implemented as a cache-write policy with a fixed
high-precision quota, tested against uniform quantization at the same memory
budget.

## Method

Model a pool of N synthetic unit vectors (tokens). Quantize most at low bits
(`b_lo`) and a protected subset (`frac` of N, e.g. 1% or 5%) at higher bits
(`b_hi`). Compare aggregate reconstruction MSE against uniform quantization at
the exact same total payload budget (interpolated log-linearly in rate between
the two measured integer-bit uniform anchors).

## Results — d=64, nrot=2000, n=1000, seed=0

```
uniform MSE anchors:  b=1: 0.358441  b=2: 0.114562  b=3: 0.033379  b=4: 0.009131

 frac  b_lo b_hi   k  avg_bits  payload   pool_mse  prot_mse  reg_mse  uni_eq   ratio
1.00%   1    3    10   1.0200    65280   0.355146  0.033405  0.358396 0.350357 1.0137
1.00%   2    4    10   2.0200   129280   0.113466  0.009133  0.114520 0.111771 1.0152
5.00%   1    3    50   1.1000    70400   0.342145  0.033389  0.358395 0.319801 1.0699
5.00%   2    4    50   2.1000   134400   0.109239  0.009115  0.114509 0.101271 1.0787
```

`uni_eq`: uniform MSE at the exact same payload budget; `ratio`:
pool_mse/uni_eq (<1 means the protected pool is better at the same budget).

## Findings

1. **Protected sinks get dramatically lower MSE** (prot_mse 0.033 vs bulk
   0.358 at b_lo=1, b_hi=3 — ~10x; 0.009 vs 0.115 at b_lo=2, b_hi=4 — ~13x).
   The protected tokens are reconstructed to b_hi quality, exactly as designed.

2. **Aggregate pool MSE is slightly WORSE than uniform at the matched budget**
   (ratio 1.01–1.08). With only 1-5% protected tokens, the memory cost of the
   protected pool (upgrading their bits) slightly exceeds the benefit in raw
   aggregate MSE: the 95-99% bulk pays the rate penalty (fewer bits left) while
   only 1-5% of tokens get the improvement.

3. **This is the expected rate-distortion tradeoff, not a failure.** The
   proposal's justification is that sinks/outliers disproportionately affect
   attention/retrieval quality — a per-token error metric, not aggregate MSE.
   The benchmark shows the cost: ~1-8% aggregate MSE penalty at the same
   budget, in exchange for 10-13x better reconstruction of the protected
   tokens. Whether that exchange pays off requires a per-token-weighted metric
   (e.g. attention-weighted error, which needs a model — P0.4, deferred).

## Implications

- Protected-token pools (P1.5) work mechanically: the protected set is
  quantized at the higher precision, at a measured 1-8% aggregate-MSE cost at
  matched budget.
- The value proposition depends on a per-token importance metric (attention
  sinks, anomalous tokens) that the statistical harness cannot evaluate — this
  is a P0.4 (model) dependency, noted for the GPU box.
- The protected-pool quota must be tiny (1-5%) to keep the aggregate penalty
  bounded, consistent with the proposal's "tiny enough that memory ratio
  barely moves" requirement.
