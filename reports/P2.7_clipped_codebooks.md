# P2.7 — Clipped or Companded Lloyd-Max Codebooks

**Status:** Implemented and benchmarked (pure NumPy, no model/GPU).
**Files:** `baseline/p27_clip.py`, `baseline/run_p27.py`
**Run:** `python3 -m baseline.run_p27 --d 64 --nrot 2000`

## Purpose

Structured rotations and real KV tensors may have heavier tails than the exact
Beta model. P2.7 tests low-bit codebooks with calibrated clipping or companding
(log/mu-law) before Lloyd-Max quantization — especially relevant at b=1 and b=2,
where a few tail coordinates can dominate error.

## Method

Three codebook designs on heavy-tailed sources (98% N(0,1)+2% N(0,100) mixture;
Student-t df=3) and on the light-tailed rotation protocol:
- plain: exact-Beta(d=64) Lloyd-Max (uncalibrated reference);
- clipped: Lloyd-Max on the source truncated at a calibrated clip tau* (scan
  quantiles, keep min-MSE);
- companded: calibrated mu-law companding (log-law equivalent);
- matched-LM: Lloyd-Max on the true law (ideal reference).

## Results — d=64, nrot=2000, ncal=50000, seed=0

```

  b       plain     clipped   companded  matched-LM
  1      2.7898      2.0801      2.0811      2.0801
  2      2.6487      0.6861      0.7138      0.6861

  b       plain     clipped   companded  matched-LM
  1      0.8634      0.5761      0.5761      0.5761
  2      0.7749      0.2957      0.2997      0.2953

Table 1b — fixed adversarial x (64 coords, mixture draw, max|x|=13.15)
  b       plain     clipped   companded
  1      3.6462      2.8474      2.8449
  2      3.4964      0.5027      0.5957

Table 2 — rotation protocol (x=e1, 2000 independent Haar rotations)
  b       plain     clipped   companded
  1      0.3587      0.3587      0.3618
  2      0.1152      0.1152      0.1205
```

## Findings

1. **On heavy tails, the plain exact-Beta codebook fails badly.** Mixture at
   b=2: plain MSE 2.65 vs clipped 0.686 — a 3.9x gap. The Beta codebook assumes
   light tails and wastes its levels on a distribution that doesn't match.

2. **Calibrated clipping reaches the matched-LM optimum** (0.6861 = matched-LM
   exactly at b=2 mixture; 0.2957 vs 0.2953 for Student-t). Clipping + Lloyd-Max
   on the truncated law is essentially optimal for heavy-tailed coordinates.

3. **Companding is close but slightly worse than clipping** on the mixture
   (0.714 vs 0.686 at b=2); on Student-t it is competitive (0.300 vs 0.296).

4. **On light tails (the rotation protocol), clipping is a no-op and safe.**
   Calibration picks clip = max|coord| (no clipping), so clipped == plain
   exactly (0.3587/0.1152). Companding degrades only slightly (0.362/0.1205).
   This is the key safety property: the calibrated clip adapts and does not
   hurt when tails are light.

## Implications

- Calibrated clipping (P2.7) is validated as a robust drop-in for heavy-tailed
  coordinate distributions: matches the ideal Lloyd-Max on heavy tails and is
  exactly the plain codebook on light tails (the rotation protocol case).
- This matters for real KV tensors, which have outlier channels (QJL Fig 2);
  the exact-Beta codebook is the right model only after a good rotation, and
  clipping protects against residual tail mismatch.
- Companding is a slightly weaker but simpler alternative; clipping is
  preferred when a calibration pass is available.
