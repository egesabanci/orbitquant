# P1.1 / P1.7 / P1.8 — Mixed Mode, Analytical Debiasing, Norm-Preserving Reconstruction

**Status:** Implemented and benchmarked (pure NumPy, no model/GPU).
**Files:** `baseline/corrections.py`, `baseline/run_corrections.py`
**Run:** `python3 -m baseline.run_corrections --d 64 --nrot 2000 --b 1`

## Purpose

These are cheap post-dequantization corrections that do not change the codebook
or bitstream, plus the full product path for comparison. P1.1 is the policy that
selects among them per layer/head:

- **P1.7 (debiased):** the MSE quantizer is biased for inner products (~2/π at
  b=1, paper Sec 3.2). A multiplicative correction factor (reciprocal of the
  measured bias ratio) makes the estimator unbiased.
- **P1.8 (norm_preserving):** renormalize the dequantized vector to the original
  norm before scoring, preserving the key norm that attention actually sees.
- **product (TurboQuant_prod):** (b−1)-bit MSE base + 1-bit QJL residual sketch,
  the paper's unbiased inner-product quantizer (Algorithm 2). At b=1 this is
  pure QJL.
- **P1.1 (mixed):** the decision rule choosing among mse / debiased / product
  per layer/head based on measured bias and error.

## Protocol

Fixed adversarial `x = e_1`, fixed `y = all_equal` (nonzero dot), averaged over
`n_rot` independent Haar-QR rotations. All four modes are benchmarked at the
SAME total bit budget (b·d bits) — for product, (b−1)·d base + d QJL bits.
Report inner-product bias ratio `E[⟨y,Q(x)⟩]/⟨y,x⟩` (1.0 = unbiased) and
per-vector MSE per mode, then the policy selection.

## Findings

1. **Plain MSE is biased** (bias ~0.63 ≈ 2/π), confirming the paper's Sec 3.2
   claim. MSE is low (0.36) but the inner-product estimate is systematically
   shrunk by ~37%.

2. **Debiasing (P1.7) achieves unbiasedness** (bias ~1.01-1.04, within noise of
   1.0) at the cost of higher MSE (0.58 vs 0.36). The multiplicative correction
   fixes the bias but scales up the reconstruction error — the expected
   tradeoff: unbiased inner products come at the price of worse MSE.

3. **Product (TurboQuant_prod) is unbiased but has the highest MSE at b=1**
   (1.55 vs 0.36). At b=1 this is pure QJL (0-bit base), which is unbiased but
   has high distortion — the known property of 1-bit sketching. The product
   path's advantage is unbiasedness without calibration, at the cost of
   variance/MSE; at higher bit budgets (b≥2) the (b−1)-bit base dominates and
   product becomes competitive (see P2.2/P2.4).

4. **Norm-preserving (P1.8) partially corrects bias** (0.80 vs 0.63) at slightly
   higher MSE (0.40 vs 0.36). It does not fully remove the bias (the norm is
   preserved but the direction is still shrunk), but improves the score estimate
   at minimal MSE cost — the proposal's stated tradeoff.

5. **The mixed-mode policy (P1.1) is now a real selector over all four modes.**
   `min_mse_unbiased` picks **debiased** (the lowest-MSE unbiased mode; product
   is unbiased but 2.7x more MSE). `min_bias_within_1.5x_mse` picks
   **norm_preserving** (cheapest bias fix within the MSE budget). The measured
   per-trial aggregate confirms the selected mode is unbiased (1.01).

## Implications

- P1.7 is the cheapest way to get unbiased scores at b=1; product is the
  calibration-free fallback but pays high MSE at 1 bit.
- P1.8 provides a minimal-cost partial fix, useful where the key norm matters
  for attention.
- The mixed-mode policy (P1.1) can be driven by these measured values: at b=1,
  debiased dominates product (same unbiasedness, lower MSE); at higher bits the
  product path should be re-measured (P2.2/P2.4 show the residual stage's value
  grows with the base budget).
